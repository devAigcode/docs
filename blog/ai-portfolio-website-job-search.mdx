## What a Portfolio Actually Needs

Start with the constraint, not the ambition. A job-search portfolio is not a gallery; it is a focused answer to a hiring manager's question. The most useful one is small: a clear target role, three to five relevant case studies, and working routes to your résumé and contact details. That is also the shape AutoCoder is built around: multi-page projects, so a focused four-page portfolio is its natural output rather than a single scrolling page.

It matters most when the quality of your work cannot be read off a job title — product and UX designers, frontend engineers, developers, writers, marketers, photographers, architects, and other project-based professionals. Build one if you need to explain how you moved from a problem to a decision and an outcome, show work that resists a single résumé bullet, give recruiters one dependable link, support a career change before your title catches up, or give context for collaborative work without claiming the whole team's output as your own.

### Four pages, one question each

Keep the navigation compact: Home, Projects, About, Contact. Each page answers a question a recruiter is already asking. In AutoCoder all four come from the same brief, and it lays out the pages and the navigation between them before you refine any copy.

| Page | Recruiter's question | What goes on it |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Who are you and what role do you want? | A short introduction, target role, selected projects, résumé link, contact route |
| Projects | Can you do the work? | Three to five relevant case studies with your role, process, decisions, and outcome |
| About | What experience and skills support your work? | A concise biography, experience summary, skill list, downloadable résumé |
| Contact | How can we reach you? | A working email link and relevant professional profiles |

## Every case study in four parts

Choose projects based on the job you want, not on recency — a product designer applying for product roles might pick one onboarding project, one research-heavy piece, one retention project, and one design-system project. Then give each one the same four-part shape:

1. **Problem.** The user or business problem, and the constraint that made it hard.
2. **My contribution.** What you personally researched, designed, tested, documented, or shipped.
3. **Outcome.** A verified result, such as an 18 percent increase in completed registration. If you have no metric, describe an observable outcome without inventing a number.
4. **Evidence.** Two or three images that let the reader follow the story: a flow, a selected interface, a comparison, a prototype, or a research synthesis.

In AutoCoder this structure goes into the brief once, and the generated Projects page carries one case study per project in the same four-part shape; you drop your own evidence images into each case study in the editor.

Remove confidential information and get permission before publishing employer or client work. If a project is fictional, label it clearly — the Maya Linford portfolio used here is a fictional demonstration, including its candidate, employers, products, and results.

## The Brief Is the Build

The quality of an AI-generated site is set by the brief, not the tool. "Make me a portfolio" returns a template; a specific brief returns a site that already has your structure, your voice, and your constraints built in. There is no single correct format — there are three, and the right one depends on how well you already know your material. All three are plain language, which is exactly what AutoCoder takes as input: a paragraph you could email a freelancer is a paragraph it can build from.

<Frame caption="Three ways to write the portfolio brief: role-first, case-study-first, or minimal">
  ![Illustration of three portfolio brief formats side by side, labeled Role-first with a person icon, Case-study-first with a briefcase icon, and Minimal with a dash icon, each shown as an outlined document](/images/blog/ai-portfolio-website-job-search/04.png "Role-first, case-study-first, and minimal AI website briefs")
</Frame>

Write the brief as if you were handing it to a freelancer: name the person, the experience level, the target role, the pages, what goes on each page, and — just as important — what must not be there.

### 1. The role-first brief

Lead with the candidate and the role. This is the most reliable default for a first pass, because it front-loads the decisions a builder would otherwise guess at.

> Build a four-page portfolio website for a fictional product designer named Maya Linford, three years of experience, applying for full-time product design roles in 2026. This is an individual job seeker's site, not a company, studio, or agency. Pages: Home (name, target role, one-sentence introduction, four featured project cards), Projects (one case study per project, each in Problem / My contribution / Outcome / Evidence form), About (short bio, skill list, a Download résumé button), Contact (email, LinkedIn, Dribbble). Clean, minimal, mobile-first. No pricing page, no services page, no client login, no empty blog.

The exclusions do as much work as the inclusions. Left unconstrained, a builder reaches for the agency-site pattern it has seen a thousand times — a services tab, a pricing block, a client portal — and you spend your first revision deleting it.

### 2. The case-study-first brief

If the work is already chosen, start there and let the site build outward from it. This tends to produce stronger Projects pages, because the substance arrives before the layout.

> I'm building a job-search portfolio around four projects: (1) an onboarding redesign that lifted completed registration 18%, (2) a six-week research study, (3) a retention experiment, (4) a design-system rebuild. Build a Projects page with a card and a full case study for each, then wrap it in a Home page with my target role and a one-sentence intro, an About page with my bio and a résumé button, and a Contact page. Minimal, mobile-first.

### 3. The minimal brief

When you plan to iterate in chat, a two-line brief gets you a structure to react to faster than a long specification. It trades precision for speed — useful for a first look, risky for the final draft.

> Build a minimal four-page portfolio for a designer targeting full-time product roles in 2026 — Home, Projects, About, Contact. Keep the copy short. We'll refine the content together.

Whichever style you use, a brief that works always names the person and experience level, the target role, the pages and what goes on each one, the case-study structure, the hard exclusions, and the visual direction. Add one line for the fictional disclaimer when the example is not real.

### Refine It in Plain Language

You do not touch the code. You tell AutoCoder what to change, in a sentence, and review the result. The edits that do the most work on a portfolio are the plain ones:

| What you want | What you type |
|---|---|
| A tighter hero | Shorten the hero headline to six words and move the Download résumé button above the fold. |
| Calmer branding | Change the accent color to a slate blue, remove the drop shadows, and reduce the corner radius. |
| Plain copy | Rewrite the About page in plain language — three short sentences, no buzzwords. |
| A grid that scans | Make the project cards a two-column grid and put the metric directly under each title. |
| A simpler contact page | Make the Contact page a single column with the email button first. |
| Honest demo labeling | Add one line in the footer noting that the demo content is fictional. |

Read every page once with a recruiter's eyes before you publish. AI builders have a house style, and part of it is overwriting: a Contact page that calls a résumé a "verified curriculum vitae dossier" makes a three-year designer sound like a law firm. Ask for plain language wherever the copy sounds like it is trying to impress.

### Check Every Screen Before You Publish

A portfolio is usually opened from an email, a messaging app, or a recruiter's screen on a phone, so mobile review is part of the build, not a final cosmetic pass. AutoCoder generates responsive layouts by default, so the work here is confirming your content at each width rather than rebuilding the page three times. Test at desktop, tablet, and phone widths, and confirm that:

- Headlines wrap cleanly and no page scrolls sideways.
- Project cards collapse to one column on narrow screens and keep readable spacing.
- Images crop without hiding interface details.
- Every project card opens the correct case study.
- The résumé button opens or downloads the intended PDF.
- Email is a working mailto link and profile URLs are correct.
- Focus states, contrast, labels, and touch targets stay usable.

### Publish and Connect a Domain

A portfolio link on a résumé has to work for months, not until the next preview expires. Publish to a real address before you send the link anywhere.

1. **Published by the editor.** Publish to a platform subdomain for now, or connect a domain you own.
2. **A subdomain is fine to start.** A stable, correctly spelled subdomain beats an expired custom domain. Keep it short and professional; this is the URL that goes on the résumé.
3. **Bring your own domain if you want permanence.** Add the domain in the site's domain settings, then create the DNS record AutoCoder shows you (typically an A record or a CNAME) at your registrar. Propagation is usually minutes, occasionally a few hours.
4. **Put the site at the root, not a folder.** `yourname.com` is easier to type into an application field — and easier to say out loud — than `yourname.com/portfolio/index`.
5. **Confirm HTTPS.** The address should load securely before it goes anywhere.
6. **Use one URL everywhere.** The same published address on your résumé, your LinkedIn, your email signature, and every application form.

After publishing, watch the two checks that fail silently: make sure the résumé PDF the About page links to is the real, current file, and that it matches the résumé you upload to applications.

Run this once your AutoCoder website is live, before the link goes anywhere.

- The target role appears clearly on the Home page.
- Three to five relevant projects are easy to find.
- Every case study explains the problem, your contribution, and the outcome.
- All claims and metrics are accurate and permitted for public use.
- Confidential information has been removed.
- The résumé PDF opens and shows the latest version.
- Email and professional-profile links point to the correct destinations.

- No link points to a temporary preview or a version-specific URL.
- The live site uses a stable published address or a connected custom domain.
