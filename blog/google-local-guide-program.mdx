*Published October 27, 2026*

The Google Local Guide program lets people contribute local knowledge to Google Maps and earn recognition for it. If you already photograph restaurants, notice incorrect opening hours, or help friends choose places to visit, it gives those habits a wider audience.

Before joining, though, set realistic expectations: points measure your contributions, and occasional benefits should not be treated as compensation for your time. This guide explains how the program works, which activities earn points, and how to contribute something other people can actually use.

![Community contributors photographing a cafe and exploring a neighborhood map](/images/blog/google-local-guide-program/cover.png)

## What Is the Google Local Guide Program?

Google officially calls the community **Local Guides**. Participants contribute reviews, photos, answers, and corrections to Google Maps. Someone looking for a restaurant, shop, or attraction can use that information to make a more informed decision.

The program is for individuals. Businesses should manage their presence through Google Business Profile rather than participate as a business account. Check Google's current eligibility and country-specific age requirements before signing up. [Source: About Local Guides](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6225846?hl=en).

For a new contributor, the useful question is straightforward: what do you know about this place that its listing does not explain yet?

## How Google Local Guide Points Work

Published contributions can earn points. Common values are listed below; the longer-review bonus uses **characters**, not words.

| Contribution | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Review | 10 |
| Review exceeding 200 characters | +10 bonus |
| Rating | 1 |
| Photo | 5 |
| Video | 7 |
| Place edit | 5 |
| Missing place added | 15 |
| Road added | 15 |

These are selected activities from [Google's official points table](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6225851?hl=en).

Use the table to understand how activity is recognized, rather than to plan repetitive uploads. A clear exterior photo may answer a visitor's question more effectively than several nearly identical pictures of the same table.

Google awards points for published content. To check your progress on a computer, open Google Maps, select **Menu → Your contributions**, and open your profile. Updates can take up to 24 hours. [Source: Contribute to Google Maps and earn points](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6304221?hl=en).

The Local Guides badge starts at Level 4. Points generally do not expire, but totals can fall when contributions become ineligible. [Source: Points, levels, and badging](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6225851?hl=en).

A badge is useful context when reading a contributor's profile. Still, evaluate each review on its details: does it describe an actual visit, explain the rating, and tell you something relevant to your plans?

## Google Local Guide Perks: What Can You Expect?

Google describes possible benefits such as early access to features and invitations to special events. Its published level table does not promise a fixed reward package at each milestone. [Source: Google's program benefits information](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6225851?hl=en).

That distinction matters if you are researching Google Local Guide rewards before joining. A higher level should not be treated as a promise of free products, travel, subscriptions, or regular discounts.

You can reasonably decide to participate because you enjoy documenting places and improving local information. If a particular offer appears in your account, read its eligibility rules and conditions before deciding whether it has value for you.

Think about what would make the activity worthwhile without an offer. Perhaps you want visitors to find a difficult entrance, or you enjoy keeping a record of restaurants you have tried. Those are more dependable reasons to keep contributing.

## How to Join Google Local Guides

Start at the [official Local Guides website](https://maps.google.com/localguides/) and follow the enrollment instructions using an eligible personal Google account.

A practical first session can be simple:

1. Choose a place you have genuinely visited recently.
2. Check whether its listing is missing useful information.
3. Write a review explaining what you experienced, or upload a relevant original photo.
4. Return to your contributions profile to check the published result.

Begin with a place you know well. You will find it easier to distinguish a useful observation from an assumption, especially when describing access, service, or opening hours.

## How to Write Reviews That Help Other People

Useful Google Maps contributions answer the questions a visitor is likely to have before arriving. For a cafe, that might mean seating, noise, the entrance, or what you ordered. For a shop, it could be product selection and how clearly prices are displayed.

Google's community guidelines encourage specific reviews based on authentic experiences and clear, relevant photos. They also warn against duplicated reviews, copied images, and unnecessary edits. [Source: Local Guides community guidelines](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/7358351?hl=en).

Instead of writing “Great cafe,” describe the detail behind your opinion. A hypothetical example would be: “The upstairs seating was quiet during my weekday visit, but the stairs were narrow.” That observation helps a reader assess whether the place suits their needs without claiming that conditions are always the same.

Keep the same care when correcting information. If you are unsure whether a business has permanently closed or simply changed its hours, verify what you can before submitting an edit.

## Mistakes to Avoid When Contributing

A review should reflect your own experience. Google prohibits paid or incentivized reviews and content affected by conflicts of interest. A business offering a free meal in exchange for a review is different from a program benefit offered by Google. [Source: Google Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en).

Avoid turning a points target into a reason to post weak material. Repeating a review across locations, uploading someone else's pictures, or making edits you cannot support reduces the usefulness of the listing.

Before publishing, check three things: is this accurate, is it relevant to this location, and would it help someone decide what to do? If a contribution fails those checks, revise it first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Google Local Guides Get Paid?

Treat Local Guides as a voluntary contribution program, not a paid reviewing job. The published program information describes points, recognition, and possible benefits rather than a wage or a cash redemption schedule. [Source: Local Guides program overview](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6225846?hl=en).

### What Does a Local Guide Badge Mean?

It indicates that the contributor has reached Level 4 or above. Read individual reviews for their substance rather than relying on the badge alone. [Source: Community guidelines](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/7358351?hl=en).

### How Can I Check My Local Guide Points?

On a computer, open Google Maps and go to **Menu → Your contributions**, then select your name to view your profile. Allow up to 24 hours for recent points to appear. [Source: Google's contribution instructions](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6304221?hl=en).

### Is the Google Local Guide Program Worth Joining?

It can be worthwhile if you enjoy sharing local knowledge and helping others choose places. If your main goal is receiving merchandise or earning money, the program's recognition system is unlikely to meet that expectation.

## Conclusion

The Google Local Guide program is most useful when your contributions answer real questions about real places. Start with one location you know, add a specific observation or a clear original photo, and build from there. Let points track your participation while usefulness guides what you publish.
