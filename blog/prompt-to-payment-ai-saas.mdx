There is a rewarding moment in every AI app project: you enter a prompt, watch the pages take shape, and see an idea from your notes running in the browser.

But a working product also needs a reliable way to make money. If you are deciding how to [accept payments for an AI app,](https://anyway.sh/blog/accept-payments-for-ai-app?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "Guide to accepting payments for an AI app") checkout is only the first requirement. [Anyway](https://anyway.sh/?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "Anyway Merchant of Record and payment links for SaaS") can handle the payment, subscription, tax, and invoicing side, while [AutoCoder](https://www.autocoder.cc/platform?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=latest&utm_campaign=PromptToPayment "Build full-stack AI SaaS apps with AutoCoder.cc") can generate the frontend, backend, database, authentication, and admin tools as one connected application.

Then the less exciting question arrives: **How will someone pay for it?**

That question usually leads to several more. You need a checkout. Subscription products need renewals and cancellations. Selling across countries brings tax into the process. And once a payment succeeds, the app must identify the customer and know which features to unlock.

**Customers may never see this work. But without it, you have a useful demo rather than a SaaS business.**

## 2. The awkward gap between built and sellable

Suppose you have created a small AI tool that turns product links into advertising concepts. New users receive three free generations, while the Pro plan is \$29 per month.

The product is easy to describe. The billing rules are harder.

What happens after a renewal fails? If a customer cancels, do they keep access through the end of the billing period? Which VAT rate applies to a customer in Germany? Who provides the invoice? And what should happen if the payment notification is delivered twice?

You may be tempted to leave these questions until later. That approach works only until your first customer tries to purchase a plan.

AI products introduce another consideration: the buyer may not always be a person moving through your website. AI agents are starting to find and buy tools for users. A long checkout designed only for human visitors could eventually limit distribution.

Few people start an AI SaaS because they want to manage payment edge cases or submit VAT returns. Keeping that work outside the core application is usually the practical choice.

## 3. Wiring the upgrade button without overengineering it

AutoCoder customers on supported plans can export the complete application, which means the payment link can be connected to the generated backend like another external service.

First, save the link in a server-side environment variable:

```bash
ANYWAY_PAYMENT_LINK=https://pay.anyway.sh/pay/PL_EXAMPLE
```

When an authenticated user selects Upgrade, build the checkout URL and include internal references for the user and order:

```ts
app.get("/upgrade", (req, res) => {
  const checkout = new URL(process.env.ANYWAY_PAYMENT_LINK!);
  checkout.searchParams.set("merchant_reference", req.user.purchaseId);
  checkout.searchParams.set("user_id", req.user.id);
  res.redirect(checkout.toString());
});
```

Use internal identifiers rather than email addresses or other personal data. Query parameters can help match an order to your database, but they are visible in the URL and must not contain secrets.

One point deserves attention from the beginning: do not activate the Pro plan only because the browser arrives at a success page. Redirects may be interrupted, changed, or replayed.

Configure [Anyway commerce webhooks](https://docs.anyway.sh/features/webhooks?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "Anyway webhooks documentation") instead. Your backend should verify the signed event, compare its amount and product with your own records, and then update the customer's access.

Store the webhook delivery ID too. Payment systems retry events, and receiving the same notification twice should not create two subscriptions or add the same credits again.

That covers most of the integration: a hosted link for checkout and a small amount of careful backend logic for fulfillment.

## 4. What to watch after launch

Receiving the first payment feels good, but it does not yet show whether the business model works.

For an AI SaaS, you need to monitor revenue beside usage cost. A \$29 monthly plan can seem profitable until one active customer generates \$25 in model costs. This is why an unlimited plan can be dangerous before you understand how customers actually use the product.

Begin with a defined allowance and follow a few basic numbers:

- how many customers reach the free limit;
- how many open the checkout;
- how many finish the payment;
- how much an active account costs to serve;
- whether paying customers renew.

You do not need perfect pricing on the first day. You need to find the point where customers see enough value to buy, while making sure you can provide that value at a sustainable cost.

### AI credits vs usage-based billing

The [AI credits vs usage-based billing](https://anyway.sh/blog/ai-credits-vs-usage-based-billing?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "Choosing between AI credits and usage-based billing") decision changes how customers understand price and how your backend grants access. Credits work well when model cost varies by task; usage billing fits measurable units customers already understand. A fixed subscription is easier to explain when usage remains predictable.

| Billing model | Best fit | Implementation check |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription | Predictable usage | Grant access for a defined billing period |
| Credits | AI jobs with variable model cost | Keep an append-only credit ledger |
| Usage-based | Clear, measurable units | Meter usage and define caps or invoices |
| Hybrid | Base access plus variable demand | Track the subscription and usage separately |

Whichever model you choose, [global subscription payments for SaaS](https://anyway.sh/blog/global-subscription-payments-for-saas?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "Handling global subscription payments for SaaS") require explicit states for the first payment, renewal, failed payment, cancellation, refund, and entitlement expiry.

AutoCoder's guide to moving [full-stack apps from prompt to production](https://www.autocoder.cc/docs/blog/prompt-to-production-full-stack-app?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=latest&utm_campaign=PromptToPayment "AutoCoder guide: full-stack apps from prompt to production") explains the other side of this problem: keeping model infrastructure dependable and tracking token expenses. Automated revenue matters, and so does understanding the cost of each customer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I accept payments for an AI app?

Create the product and price, send buyers to a hosted checkout, and use signed webhooks to grant access after a verified payment. A service built to [accept payments for an AI app](https://anyway.sh/blog/accept-payments-for-ai-app?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "How to accept payments for an AI app with Anyway") can also cover recurring billing, invoices, and the tax workflow.

### Can I sell SaaS without a company?

Some payment platforms let eligible individuals or sole proprietors start selling before incorporating. The exact answer depends on identity checks, country availability, tax residence, product eligibility, and payout requirements. Review the provider's current rules before you [sell SaaS without a company.](https://anyway.sh/blog/sell-saas-without-a-company?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "Selling SaaS as an individual without a company")

### Does an AI SaaS product need a Merchant of Record?

Not always. A [Merchant of Record for AI SaaS](https://anyway.sh/blog/merchant-of-record-for-ai-saas?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "When an AI SaaS needs a Merchant of Record") is most useful when you want one party to manage the covered transaction, sales tax or VAT, invoices, refunds, and global payment operations while your team focuses on the product.

### What billing model fits an AI SaaS product?

Use a fixed subscription when customer usage and your model costs are predictable. Use credits when each AI job has a different cost, or usage-based billing when buyers understand the metered unit. The right [AI credits vs usage-based billing](https://anyway.sh/blog/ai-credits-vs-usage-based-billing?utm_source=autocoder&utm_medium=guest_blog&utm_campaign=prompt_to_payment "AI credits versus usage-based billing for AI SaaS") model should keep price understandable and gross margin measurable.
