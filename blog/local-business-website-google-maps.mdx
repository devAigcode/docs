That creates a practical service opportunity. Instead of pitching a website as an abstract idea, you can build a working preview first, show the owner something concrete, and offer to customize and launch it.

## Step 1: Find a business with strong proof but a weak web presence

Open Google Maps and search for a local service category in a specific city—for example, "barber shops El Paso TX."

Look for a business that has:

- A healthy number of reviews
- A rating high enough to establish trust
- Several useful customer or owner photos
- Complete opening hours and contact information
- No independent website, or only a social profile or third-party booking page

Clipperhands Barbershop WESTSIDE was a good fit because the listing already contained 271 reviews, a 4.6 rating, business hours, phone details, and a large photo gallery. Its "website" destination was a Booksy page rather than a standalone branded site.

## Step 7: Customize before outreach

The generated preview is the starting point, not the finished client deliverable. Before contacting the owner:

1. Check the business name, address, phone number, hours, and booking link.
2. Remove or rewrite any unsupported claim.
3. Make sure customer reviews are attributed correctly and shown exactly as sourced.
4. Test the layout on desktop and mobile.
5. Confirm that all buttons lead to the intended destination.
6. Decide what you would add in the paid version, such as a custom domain, service menu, contact form, analytics, SEO setup, or direct booking integration.

## Why this approach works

A cold pitch asks the owner to imagine the result. A preview lets them see it.

Google Maps already contains the raw material: social proof, photos, hours, location, and customer language. AutoCoder turns those pieces into a structured website draft, so you can spend more time refining the offer and less time starting from a blank page.

The preview starts the conversation. The service—verification, customization, launch, and ongoing support—is what you sell.

---

## Important checks before using this workflow commercially

- Confirm that the business does not already have an independent website under another domain.
- Treat Maps information as a lead signal, not proof that every detail is current.
- Ask the owner to approve business facts, photos, testimonials, branding, and final copy before publishing.
- Respect the applicable rights and platform terms for photos and review content.
- Do not imply that the preview was commissioned or approved by the business.
- Keep the draft private until the owner authorizes publication.
